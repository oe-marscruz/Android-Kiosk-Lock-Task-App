### §3.3 — Step-up for `wipe` and `exit_kiosk`

These operations remain disabled unless `DESTRUCTIVE_ACTIONS_ENABLED` is true and P5b evidence for the current issuer, RP and policy is recorded. All §3.3 authorization, attachment, consent and audit requirements also apply; confirmation does not substitute for them.

1. The desktop displays the exact device and operation and obtains explicit confirmation. It creates fresh independent `state_d`, `verifier_d`, `challenge_d` and the exclusive loopback listener as in §3.1 step 1. With the current session bearer in the HTTPS Authorization header, it sends `POST /auth/step-up/transactions` JSON `{device_id, op, port, state_d, challenge_d, confirmed: true}`. Reject duplicate or unknown fields, malformed values and any op other than `wipe` or `exit_kiosk`; validate port and binding values as in §3.1. Authenticate the unexpired, unrevoked session and check current admin authorization, device scope, applicable policy and flag. The confirmation asserts the desktop UI action; it is not independent proof of human presence. Bind the transaction to the principal, original session ID, device, exact op and confirmation. Never accept a client-supplied principal or redirect URI.
2. Atomically permit at most one unexpired pending transaction per `(session_id, device_id, op)` through redemption; a competing initiation fails, with no replacement. Mint a random 256-bit `start_id`, expire it after 5 minutes and return `{start_url}` as in §3.1. Use §3.1's per-transaction browser cookie, CSRF form, exact Origin check, atomic continuation, independent OIDC state, nonce and S256 PKCE, confidential-client code exchange and fixed callback. The form identifies the device and op. Include `max_age=0` in the authorization request. Duo supports `prompt=login`, but it is redundant and is not sent: Duo documents it as equivalent to `max_age=0`. Neither setting alone proves fresh MFA; tenant policy and P5 evidence must establish that property. [Duo Generic OIDC](https://duo.com/docs/sso-oidc-generic); [OIDC Core §3.1.2.1](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0.html#AuthRequest).
3. Callback atomically claims the unexpired transaction, validates the cookie and OIDC response and performs all §3.1 ID-token checks. Require the exact original `(iss, sub)`, accepted `amr`, and numeric `auth_time` no earlier than initiation, at most 5 minutes old and no more than the configured bounded clock skew in the future. OIDC requires `auth_time` when `max_age` is used; Duo's Generic RP guide does not document that claim. Missing, malformed, stale or unsuitable claims fail closed. The fresh primary authentication timestamp and AMR alone do not prove a fresh second factor; P5a/P5b must establish it for the effective policy, including remembered devices and bypass.
4. After validation, mint a random 256-bit single-use handoff code, valid for at most 60 seconds and no longer than the transaction or freshness deadline, bound to the transaction and `challenge_d`. Redirect to the literal `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/cb` with only code and `state_d` as in §3.1. The desktop validates path, unique parameters and state, closes the listener on success, cancellation or 5-minute timeout, and retains the original bearer and verifier in memory only. No Duo token reaches the desktop.
5. Desktop sends `POST /auth/step-up/token` with the **original session bearer** and JSON `{code, verifier_d}` over HTTPS. Atomically validate the unused code, S256 verifier and same live session and principal; recheck `auth_time` freshness, current authorization, applicable policy and flag. A wrong verifier does not consume a valid code. On success consume the code and return a random opaque 256-bit `step_up_id`, stored hashed and bound to principal, session, device, op, confirmation and `auth_time`. Concurrent redemption yields at most one grant. Its expiry is the earliest of 5 minutes after `auth_time` and session expiry; idle expiry and revocation also invalidate it. Close the pending transaction on successful redemption, failure, cancellation or expiry.
6. The command carries `step_up_id`. Immediately before forwarding, recheck the live session, authorization, policy, attachment/consent, flag, confirmation and freshness. Atomically consume the matching unused grant and record the dispatch authorization audit before forwarding; mismatched, expired or replayed grants deny dispatch. Serialize consumption and relevant policy/revocation changes so concurrent requests permit at most one dispatch. If audit or authoritative state is unavailable, fail closed. A consumed grant is never restored after a failed or uncertain dispatch; retry requires a new confirmation and step-up.

Transactions, codes and grants are invalidated on session logout, expiry or revocation, relevant authorization or policy removal, or flag disablement. Expire abandoned state; rate-limit initiation, continuation and redemption. On browser cancellation, timeout, invalid callback, failed exchange, wrong identity, denied policy or unavailable services, issue no grant and require a new flow. Use §3.1 no-store/no-referrer headers, TLS and redaction for all auth traffic. Audit initiation, denial, callback outcome, redemption, grant consumption and dispatch outcome with principal, session, device, op and reason, without bearer, code, verifier, state, cookie, token or query string; audit failure prevents authorization or dispatch.

## Acceptance tests:

- **Mock IdP and relay:** Exercise authenticated initiation through browser binding, `max_age=0`, callback, loopback and redemption with the original bearer and verifier; assert exact bindings and one dispatch. Reject missing/wrong bearer, revoked or expired session, malformed fields, false confirmation, wrong op/device, missing role/scope, disabled flag and missing P5b evidence. Check cookie/CSRF/Origin/state/nonce/PKCE, duplicate inputs, expiry and callback replay.
- Reject wrong issuer/subject, missing or unaccepted AMR, missing/malformed/stale/future `auth_time`; change authorization, session or flag between each stage and before dispatch. Verify wrong verifier preserves the code, another session cannot redeem it, duplicate initiation conflicts, concurrent redemption and dispatch succeed at most once, and expired/replayed/mismatched grants fail. Simulate audit failure, cancellation, disconnect and uncertain dispatch; inspect logs for secrets.
- **P5a:** With trial Duo and local relay, record sanitized claim and timing evidence, exercise interactive step-up and remembered-device, bypass and denied-AMR cases using a harmless substitute. Establish whether a fresh second factor actually occurs; keep destructive flag off.
- **P5b:** Repeat with CU OIT for the actual issuer, RP and policy, including remembered-device, bypass and unmapped/revoked access; record claim semantics and fresh MFA evidence. Missing evidence or failed negative checks keeps the flag off. Test real destructive operations only on an explicitly authorized disposable device.
